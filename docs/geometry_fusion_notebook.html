Where we stand

Additive> LoRA only> Cross-attention

First positive geometry result. On the full VSI-Bench (5,130 questions, 32 frames), each arm at its best-dev checkpoint: additive 55.0, LoRA 52.7, cross-attention 49.0. Additive − LoRA +2.4 [+0.9, +3.8], close to SpatialStack's published +2.8 (4B, full fine-tuning). Cross-attention − LoRA −3.7 [−5.1, −2.2]; cross-attention − additive −6.1 [−7.5, −4.6]. Intervals: 95% paired bootstrap, stratified by task.

On Qwen3.5-4B the additive gain shrinks to a tie: additive 59.0, LoRA 58.7, cross-attention 51.0. Additive − LoRA +0.3 [−1.1, +1.6]; cross-attention − LoRA −7.7 [−9.1, −6.3]. The 4B LoRA control keeps appearance-order ability that the 2B loses (50.6 vs 24.8), and additive gives up 15 points on that one task; on the other seven tasks additive leads by about 2.5. Scaling 2B → 4B is worth +6.0 [+4.7, +7.4] for LoRA and +3.9 [+2.4, +5.3] for additive.

  • Additive gains exactly where geometry should help: relative direction +11.6, room size +9.8, absolute distance +8.4. It loses on appearance order (−9.1), the order-dependent task we flagged at design time: it has no training data and VGGT is permutation-equivariant beyond the first frame.
  • The lower fusion lr is what changed the additive result. Its injection ratio now settles at 0.13–0.25 (v2: 0.4–0.6), and its dev accuracy is at or above LoRA's at every epoch. On held-out scenes there is no sign of the scene-fingerprint shortcut proposed after v2.
  • Cross-attention no longer runs away, and its gradient spikes are a warmup artefact, not persistent instability. All 70 (2B) and 78 (4B) steps with gradient norm above 50 fall inside the 559-step warmup; the last is at step 493 / 512, and after warmup its median gradient norm (3.95 / 3.12) matches additive's (3.92 / 3.00). The giant first-step norms (6,469 / 7,294 vs about 250–350 for the other arms) come from the zero-initialised output projections: their gradient is large before the block has any scale, and clipping at 1.0 absorbs it. Every arm shares the same 10–12 spikes at steps 1–12. Corrected 2026-10-27; an earlier version of this page called the arm “still unstable”.
  • Injection ratio is a per-video quantity, and one training video doubles it. The logged ratio is from the last micro-batch of each step, so the curve is per-video noise around a slow trend. The three largest jumps (steps 4165, 4893, 5558: mean ratio 0.21 → 0.37, point 2 from 0.17 to 0.46 for additive; point 1 from 0.21 to 0.42 for cross-attention) are the same video every epoch, scene0152_00, a dark office with large uniform carpet and blank walls, in both the 2B and 4B runs. The likely mechanism is small Qwen hidden-state norms on low-texture regions rather than large VGGT features; the activation study below would settle it.
  • Correlations (log-based, small n). Across the six runs, dev accuracy at epoch 6 tracks full-VSI score (r = +0.87), so dev selection is doing its job. Within each geometry run, the dev-accuracy advantage over the size-matched LoRA grows as the injection ratio opens (additive r = +0.70, cross-attention r = +0.84 over 12 epoch-points each), but pooled across arms the correlation is zero: cross-attention sits at a higher ratio with a lower advantage. More injection is not better in itself; what the vector contains is. Per task on dev, additive's advantage is concentrated in relative direction (+11 at both sizes) and absolute distance; it loses on counting at 4B (−6.3).
  • Training may be stopping early. All three arms peak at the final epoch. LoRA's dev accuracy still rose 3.5 points in epoch 6; additive and cross-attention flattened (+0.1).
  • The 1,000-question subset is too noisy for differences this small. On it, v3 scores LoRA 55.2, additive 54.6, cross-attention 50.4, so the subset would have called LoRA vs additive the other way. Final comparisons should use full VSI-Bench. Against v2 on the same subset, every arm improved (LoRA +2.2, additive +5.4, cross-attention +6.7).
  • Dev accuracy (70–75) is far above VSI (49–59): dev is in-distribution ScanNet with the six training tasks; VSI adds ARKitScenes, ScanNet++ and two unseen tasks.
  • Appearance order decides the 4B headline. The 4B control keeps order reasoning (50.6) and the geometry arms lose it (35.4 / 19.3). This matches VLM-3R and MVRD, which both found VGGT-based injection or distillation hurts appearance order: VGGT is permutation-equivariant beyond the first frame and the injected features carry no frame order. A frame-index embedding on the geometry tokens, or appearance-order training data, is the obvious fix before any further comparison.
  • Cross-attention is consistently 6–8 points behind additive at both sizes, with the same instability signature (gradient-norm spikes). The literature review found no precedent for our design's query/key space; the JE-xattn proposal addresses exactly this.

Run v2 findings

LoRA only> Additive> Cross-attention

The reverse of the hypothesis. On the 1,000-question VSI subset at 32 frames, epoch 10: additive is 3.7 points below LoRA [−6.5, −0.9] and cross-attention 9.3 below [−12.7, −5.9]. MMSI is flat (24–26%) for all arms.

Baselines. “LoRA only” is the fine-tuned 2B control: same data, schedule and LoRA, no geometry. Zero-shot Qwen3.5-2B on the same 1,000 questions scores 36.2 at 32 frames, 28.7 at 8 frames and 26.3 on MMSI, so every fine-tuned arm is far above zero-shot on VSI. Much of that jump is answer format: zero-shot often writes reasoning instead of an option letter, for example in 58 of 100 appearance-order answers.

  • Opening the geometry path costs 11–14 points at epoch 1. Additive climbs back to within about 3.5 points by epochs 5–8; cross-attention stalls about 8 points down.
  • The two geometry arms fail differently. At epoch 10, cross-attention loses almost entirely on the two relational multiple-choice tasks (relative distance −27, relative direction −19 vs LoRA), which are also the two tasks with the most training data. Additive loses a few points on most tasks but gains on room size (+9; 61% of answers within 20% of the truth vs 47% for LoRA) and slightly on relative direction. Some geometric signal is getting through.
  • Cross-attention had a runaway in epoch 3 and never recovered. Its injection ratio doubled (0.6 → 1.4 at layer 0) during the gradient blow-up around step 1,580 and stayed there. From then on the block writes a vector 1.5× the residual stream into every token, text included, at layers 0–2. The v2 cross-attention result is therefore not a fair test of the design.
  • Additive generalises worse from the very first epoch. In epoch 1 its training loss tracks LoRA's (0.99 vs 0.92), yet VSI is 11 points lower, evenly across ScanNet, ARKitScenes and ScanNet++ (so not a VGGT domain shift). Two candidate causes: VGGT features act as a per-scene fingerprint that makes memorising the 1,201 training videos easier (the dev split tests this), or rank-64 LoRA cannot re-adapt layers 3–23 to the altered visual tokens the way full fine-tuning can.
  • Appearance order is not the explanation. LoRA's 67 at epoch 1 is a transient; by epoch 2 every arm sits at or below chance (25 / 16 / 24 at epoch 10), and all arms over-predict “A”. Fine-tuning on VLM-3R destroys this ability regardless of geometry. (Corrected 2026-10-26: an earlier version of this page blamed the gap on appearance order.)
  • Training loss ranks the arms backwards and cannot pick a winner. LoRA and additive memorise the training set from about epoch 6; LoRA's best VSI score is at epoch 1.
  • Our recipe differs from SpatialStack's in ways that likely matter: 10× the learning rate on everything, 10 epochs instead of 1, a narrow 15.8k-question dataset, and LoRA instead of full fine-tuning.
  • Best-epoch numbers are chosen on the test set. There was no dev split in v2; one now exists for the next run.
How the arms differ

Architecture

All arms share the frozen Qwen vision encoder, frozen VGGT and LoRA (r64) on the LLM. They differ only in how geometry from three VGGT layers reaches the first three LLM layers.

2026-10-24 · superseded design

Pilot (3 epochs)

VSI at 32 frames, epoch 3: LoRA 56.6, cross-attention 56.5, additive 42.5.

The pilot used one shared module injected before all six softmax-attention layers (3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23), VGGT layer 23 only, 3% warmup with cosine to zero. Cross-attention used Flamingo tanh gates. Those gates never opened, so the cross-attention arm was effectively LoRA. The additive arm was not a faithful SpatialStack control. Both problems led to the v2 redesign.

2026-10-26

Run v3: all three arms, bigger cap, split learning rates, dev-selected final eval done

Changes applied to every arm: per-task cap 6,000 (about 26k questions vs 15.8k; the three largest tasks double, the three smallest are unchanged), LoRA lr 1e-4 with the new modules at 1e-5 (SpatialStack's merger lr), weight decay 0.01 on matrices only, 6 epochs (about the same optimizer-step count as v2's 10). Dev split as below. The checkpoint with the highest dev accuracy is evaluated on the full VSI-Bench (5,130 questions) at 32 frames, in a job queued with a Slurm dependency so it starts the moment training ends. No per-epoch subset evals, no 8-frame or MMSI eval. Jobs 206880–206885; 4.1–5.1 h training per arm. Result: additive 55.0 > LoRA 52.7 > cross-attention 49.0 on full VSI. On Qwen3.5-4B (jobs 206907–206913, 8.4–9.3 h per arm): additive 59.0 ≈ LoRA 58.7 > cross-attention 51.0.

2026-10-26

Cross-attention stays ungated in v3 adopted

The runaway is addressed by the 10× lower learning rate on the new modules rather than a gate, so the additive and cross-attention arms stay structurally matched. If the injection ratio runs away again, a bounded gate is the next step.

Add a held-out dev split built

60 whole videos held out (scene-level), up to 100 dev questions per task. Dev loss at step 0, every 200 steps and at each epoch end; dev accuracy (VSI scoring) at step 0 and each epoch end. Checkpoints will be chosen on dev, not on VSI. Smoke test passed.

2026-10-24 eve

Additive arm = SpatialStack recipe adopted

VGGT 11/17/23 → three separate mergers → added after LLM layers 0/1/2. Replaces the pilot's shared merger at six layers.

2026-10-24 eve

Cross-attention at the same points, no gate adopted

Same VGGT layers and injection points as additive; one resampler and one xattn+FFW block per point. Tanh gates replaced by zero-initialised output projections, because the pilot's gates never opened.

2026-10-24 eve

No weight sharing across injection points adopted

Each depth gets its own merger or resampler, as in SpatialStack.

2026-10-24 eve

10 epochs, LoRA, lr 1e-4; warmup 10%, cosine to 0.1 × peak adopted

Longer training to let the model adapt to a new modality. LoRA kept for cost. Under review: v2 shows memorisation from epoch 6 and LoRA peaking at epoch 1.

2026-10-24

Frames as images, VGGT on the fly adopted

Each frame is a separate image (SpatialStack's protocol; needed for per-frame alignment with VGGT). One video per micro-batch, 4 micro-batches per step; VGGT runs inside the model, no feature cache.

Gave LoRA 56.6, xattn 56.5 (gates closed), additive 42.5. Replaced by the v2 design.

proposed

JE-xattn: a JEPA-aligned shared query/key space for the cross-attention arm proposal

2×2-window VGGT keys on Qwen's token grid plus a predictive alignment loss between LLM hidden states and the geometry embedding of the same region. Design, the 2×2 arithmetic, intuition, monitors and a 4-GPU-hour pilot plan: JE-xattn Proposal. A literature review of about 100 papers found no prior work combining the two; it recommends a frozen rather than EMA target, reading the loss at a middle layer, one shared predictor, and a frame-index embedding on the keys. Nothing built or run.

70 gradient-norm spikes above 50 even at fusion lr 1e-5. Candidates: a bounded tanh gate with a small non-zero init, a lower lr for the blocks only, or tighter clipping on the fusion parameters.

One short GPU job on the cached SpatialStack-Qwen3.5-4B: if its mergers add about 0.1× the hidden state where ours add 0.5×, our mergers opened too far.
