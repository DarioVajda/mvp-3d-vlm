JE-xattn Proposal

A shared embedding space inside the geometry cross-attention, defined by a JEPA-style predictive loss between what the language model sees at an image region and VGGT's geometry of the same region.

Status: proposal only. Nothing has been built or run. Run v3 (2B and 4B, all three arms) is training; its results decide whether this is worth doing.

The problem this addresses

The answer loss is too sparse to teach geometry

Every fusion module we have trained so far learns only from the answer loss: one letter or one number per question, about 25k questions on 1,201 scenes. That signal is easy to satisfy without using geometry.

Run v2 showed the symptom. In epoch 1 the additive arm reached the same training loss as LoRA-only (0.99 vs 0.92) yet scored 11 points lower on held-out VSI scenes. The cross-attention arm, whose queries and 256 latents share no notion of image location, ran away in epoch 3 and lost most on the two relational tasks (relative distance −27, relative direction −19 vs LoRA).

The proposal adds a second, dense training signal: at every visual token of every frame, the LLM's hidden state must be able to predict the geometry embedding of the same image region. No labels, no scene identity to memorise, and a target that changes as the camera moves. The same embeddings serve as the cross-attention's keys, so the attention block starts with a built-in notion of "the geometry of the place I'm looking at".

The arithmetic behind the window

The 2×2 math: why the grids coincide exactly

Two encoders with different patch sizes see the same frame. The window factor, the VGGT input resolution and the token index are all forced by requiring that one geometry token covers exactly the pixels of one Qwen visual token.

The objective

A JEPA-style predictive loss defines the shared space

JEPA, in one sentence: learn a representation by predicting the embedding of something you cannot see from the embedding of something you can, rather than predicting pixels. Both sides map into one space (joint embedding); the loss is a regression in that space (predictive), not a contrast against negatives.

Why predictive, not reconstructive
VGGT's 2048-d features carry a lot that is irrelevant to answering questions (register noise, view-specific detail). Predicting a learned embedding lets the target side discard what is unpredictable, which is V-JEPA's argument against pixel reconstruction. The shared space can settle on the stable geometric content of a region.
Why not contrastive
InfoNCE would push region i away from region i+1. Neighbouring regions on the same wall genuinely share geometry, and the space should reflect that. A regression loss has no such pressure.
Why it does not collapse
The target is a slowly moving EMA copy with stop-gradient. If the context side output a constant, the target would keep moving and the loss would not stay low. The predictor absorbs the "which parts are predictable" question so the space itself does not have to. This is the BYOL / V-JEPA mechanism; its known failure (EMA too fast) is monitorable.
Where the analogy stops
Meta's JEPAs pre-train a video encoder from scratch. We are not learning geometry; VGGT has it. We use the JEPA objective as an alignment signal between a frozen geometry encoder and a language model, closer in role to LLaVA's projector pre-training, with the JEPA machinery borrowed to make the alignment dense and collapse-resistant.
Why this suits geometric encoding

A prediction task you can only solve by understanding geometry

Ask what the LLM's hidden state at region i would need to contain to predict region i's geometry embedding. Not pixels, not colour, not the object's name. It needs whatever varies with depth, surface shape and camera pose. The only way to drive the loss down is to put geometric information into the hidden state at the visual positions, in a form that lines up with VGGT's notion of it.

That is the capability the answer loss failed to install. The alignment loss supplies it at every one of the 2,400 visual tokens of every video, labelled by region, with no shortcut through scene identity: the target for region i changes as the camera moves, and there are no answers to memorise.

Instrumentation

What we would watch, and what would tell us it failed

Two honest risks. VGGT layer 11 still carries texture, so the target space could partly encode appearance, which would make alignment partly trivial; the dev retrieval check and the probe would show that. And the EMA target drifts during training, so the keys change under the attention block; the injection-ratio and retrieval curves would show whether that destabilises it.

Budget

Cost of trying it on a small subset

2B model, one B300. v3's cross-attention arm runs at 3.1 s/step with 256 keys; 2,400 keys plus the alignment MLP should give about 3.5–4 s/step and a few GB more than v3's 26 GB peak.

What the literature says about this design

Nobody has combined a latent-prediction loss on LLM hidden states with geometry cross-attention keys. The two halves exist separately: “inject geometry, next-token loss only” (SpatialStack, VLM-3R, VG-LLM, Spatial-MLLM, GUIDE, Stream3D-VLM, SpaceMind) and “align hidden states to frozen VGGT, discard at inference” (3DRS, Spatial Forcing, MVRD, GLaD). The closest prior work is Spa3R (arXiv 2602.21186): JEPA-style L1 + cosine prediction of frozen VGGT and DINOv3 features at novel views, done outside the LLM, whose latents then become K/V of a zero-init cross-attention adapter. Its ablations: cross-attention 58.6 vs appending 51.1 VSI-Bench; predicted latents 58.6 vs raw VGGT 55.1; VGGT + DINOv3 58.6 vs VGGT alone 57.5. JARVIS (2512.15885) is the one JEPA inside an LLM: frozen DINOv2 target, the LLM's first quarter as predictor, best at layer 8 of 32 only.

Revisions this suggests (not yet adopted; see open decisions): (1) make the target a frozen projection of the VGGT window, with EMA as an ablation rather than the default; (2) keep injection at layers 0–2 but read the alignment loss from a middle layer (around 8–12 of 24), where it demonstrably helps, instead of at the injection layers; (3) share one predictor across injection points; (4) add a frame-index embedding to the keys; (5) pre-register appearance order, room size and MMSI as regression gates; (6) consider mixing a semantic target (DINOv3) with VGGT, as Spa3R and VaLR do.
