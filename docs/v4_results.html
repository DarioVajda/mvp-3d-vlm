Verdict

Additive still wins (63.5), but aligned cross-attention with both position signals is now within 1.8 points (61.7) and 3.9 above LoRA (57.8). The old resampler-based cross-attention arms sit at 54.7–55.5, below LoRA. The aligned design's gain comes entirely from position: without any position signal the attention is uniform over all 2,400 geometry tokens (entropy 7.6 of a maximum 7.8 nats, own-frame mass exactly 1/8) and the run scores at LoRA level. The locality bias alone lifts it to 59.2; RoPE₃ on top of the bias adds another 2.5. RoPE₃ alone does nothing (58.3, still uniform attention). The learned bias scalars drifted sharper (λ 1.5 → 1.7), toward the additive arm's one-hot alignment, and the aligned arm's remaining deficit is on relative direction, the task most sensitive to exact token-to-geometry correspondence.

Interpretation

1. Correspondence, not attention, was the missing ingredient
The v2–v4 cross-attention arms attended from every token to 256 location-free latents and lost 8–9 points to additive. Giving the keys a location (one per visual token) and starting the attention on the matching key recovers 6 of those points. The no-position aligned run, which has the same keys but no way to find the matching one, scores exactly like LoRA: a uniform average of the video's geometry is worth nothing to the LLM.
2. The attention never discovered correspondence on its own
Two runs had the information needed to localise (RoPE₃ gives relative position; the keys are on the same grid) and 7,600 steps to learn it, and stayed uniform. With W_o at zero the gradient to W_q and W_k is zero at step 0 and tiny after, and uniform attention is a flat point for the content logits. The bias breaks the symmetry; RoPE₃ then adds 2.5 points on top, so relative position is useful once something anchors it.
3. Training wanted a sharper prior
λ increased at two of three points, self mass rose through training, and additive (one-hot) still leads. The design question "does a soft alignment beat a fixed one?" currently answers "not yet, and the optimiser is pulling toward fixed". Two readings are possible: the soft alignment is genuinely worse, or the start was too wide (self mass 0.32) and the slow lr on λ could not take it where it wanted to go. The per-head spread from the λ/μ report (λ from 0.18 to 4, μ from 0 to 6) tests the second reading directly.
4. The remaining gap is task-specific
Aligned loses to additive on relative direction (77.6 vs 84.9), route planning (36.6 vs 42.8) and absolute distance (47.1 vs 50.3), and wins on relative distance (60.8 vs 58.7). Relative direction asks "from A facing B, where is C": it needs the geometry of specific tokens, undiluted. Relative distance compares two distances and may benefit from a token seeing its neighbours' geometry. This is the first sign of the two mechanisms having different strengths, which is the result the thesis is after; it needs a second seed before it is more than a sign.
5. The fusion learning rate is a first-order choice for additive
3e-5 instead of 1e-5 costs 3.4 points overall, and the loss is almost all off-domain (ScanNet 65.5 vs 65.3, ARKitScenes 55.2 vs 61.2). The mergers overfit the 1,141 training scenes when opened faster. v3's 1e-5 was the right call and SpatialStack's merger lr is 1e-5 too.
6. Appearance-order data removed the confound, and additive now beats SpatialStack's own geometry increment
With order data in the mix, additive − LoRA is +5.7 at the 2B, against SpatialStack's published +2.8 at the 4B with full fine-tuning. Part of that is the weaker control (our LoRA control is 57.8 vs their data-only 64.8), so the increment is easier to earn; the per-task pattern (relative direction +17.9, room size +13.8, absolute distance +8.4) is nonetheless the clearest geometry signal the project has produced.
7. Dev is a good selector but a biased ranker
Dev picked the last checkpoint in 8 of 9 runs, matching VSI in every case where it mattered. But dev ranks bias-only (73.9) above RoPE₃+bias (72.4) and additive (72.8), while VSI ranks it fourth. Dev is in-domain ScanNet, and the bias-only arm is the one whose off-domain scores fall most. A held-out ARKitScenes/ScanNet++ dev slice would fix the ranking bias.

What to try next

  • Per-head spread of λ and μ (λ ∈ {4, 1.41, 0.5, 0.18} × μ ∈ {6, 4, 2, 0}, as in the λ/μ report), same recipe otherwise. Tests whether the aligned arm closes the last 1.8 points when some heads start near one-hot. One 7 h run.
  • Second seed for additive 1e-5 and aligned RoPE₃+bias. The per-task split (direction vs distance) is the thesis's most interesting claim and rests on one seed. Two 7 h runs.
  • Evaluation-time μ correction (μ + ln(31/7) at 32 frames). Free: re-run the full-VSI eval on the existing RoPE₃+bias checkpoint. If the arm was leaking attention to the 31 other frames at evaluation, this recovers points on no training.
  • Off-domain dev slice. Hold out some ARKitScenes or ScanNet++ videos for dev so checkpoint ranking stops favouring ScanNet-fitted arms. Data change only.
  • Longer training (8 epochs) for additive and the best aligned run; both still rising at epoch 5.
  • Camera/register tokens as extra keys for the aligned arm: the one input additive cannot use, and the natural route to the relative-direction gap.
