# JEPA-aligned geometry keys remain untested ground

No published work uses a JEPA-style (or any latent-prediction) objective to pull a language model's hidden states toward a 3D geometry encoder's features *and* reuses those same embeddings as cross-attention keys; as of October 2026 the two halves of the JE-xattn idea exist only in separate papers. Latent prediction onto frozen VGGT features has been done, but outside the LLM (Spa3R, which then cross-attends the predicted latents under next-token loss alone) or as training-only supervision of LLM hidden states with no geometry input at inference (3DRS, Spatial Forcing, MVRD, GLaD). JEPA machinery inside an LLM exists once (JARVIS, with a *frozen* DINOv2 target and the LLM's first quarter as predictor) and no MLLM paper anywhere uses an EMA target. The surrounding evidence is unusually consistent on four points: injection into early LLM layers (0–5) works only with gating; alignment losses work best at middle-to-deep layers with cosine and λ≈0.5 and collapse when spread over several layers with independent projectors; every cross-modal JEPA that used a frozen teacher dropped EMA without collapse while the one that added an EMA branch collapsed; and compressing keys into location-free latents costs 10–30 points on spatial and OCR subsets. For the JE-xattn arm this means the grid-aligned 2×2 keys, zero-init gating, λ≈0.5 and the predictive (not contrastive) form are well supported; the EMA target, the alignment at layers 0–2, and three independent alignment points are each contradicted or unsupported by the closest evidence; and raw VGGT as a regression target has hurt VSI-Bench appearance order and room size in two independent VLM studies, so those sub-tasks are the canaries.

## Nobody has tied a latent-prediction loss to cross-attention keys inside an LLM

The literature splits into two camps that never overlap. Injection papers feed geometry into the model and train with next-token loss only; alignment papers regress hidden states onto a geometry teacher and discard the teacher at inference. SpatialStack states it plainly: "No auxiliary objectives or task-specific losses are introduced" ([SpatialStack](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27437v3)), and the same holds for VLM-3R, VG-LLM, Spatial-MLLM, GUIDE and Stream3D-VLM ([VLM-3R](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20279v5); [VG-LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24625v3); [Spatial-MLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23747); [GUIDE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05695); [Stream3D-VLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06891)). Conversely, 3DRS, Spatial Forcing, MVRD, GLaD and Think-with-3D all regress LLM states onto frozen VGGT features but never inject VGGT as input ([3DRS](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01946v2); [Spatial Forcing](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12276v2); [MVRD](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10864v1); [GLaD](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09619); [3DThinker](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18632)). No paper combines the two, so the interaction between an injected geometry pathway and a hidden-state alignment loss on that pathway is untested.

The single closest design is **Spa3R / Spa3-VLM**. Its encoder is pre-trained with a JEPA-inspired "predictive spatial field" objective: a 6-layer, 256-query encoder and a camera-conditioned decoder regress **frozen VGGT final-layer patch tokens and frozen DINOv3 patch tokens at novel target views** with a per-patch L1 plus cosine loss, no EMA, no stop-gradient, no variance regulariser. The frozen Spa3R latent is then read by Qwen2.5-VL-3B through a zero-initialised residual cross-attention adapter whose queries are the VLM's own visual tokens and whose keys/values are the 256 latents, trained with next-token loss only ([Spa3R](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21186v2)). Its ablations are the best available evidence that predicted geometry latents make better keys than raw ones: **cross-attention 58.6 vs sequence appending 51.1** VSI-Bench (Table VI); **Spa3R latents 58.6 vs raw VGGT features through the same adapter 55.1 vs no spatial branch 50.9** (Table V); and the target mix matters, with VGGT+DINOv3 at 58.6 beating VGGT alone 57.5, DINOv3 alone 56.7 and CLIP 51.9 (Table IV). What Spa3R does not do is put the loss on the LLM's hidden states, tie the target to the keys, or let the LLM act as predictor.

The one JEPA objective that lives inside an LLM is **JARVIS**, which runs I-JEPA within LLaVA: masked CLIP context patches plus learnable target tokens pass through the LLM, whose **first quarter of layers (j = 8 of 32) acts as the predictor**, and the loss is negative cosine against a **frozen DINOv2-L target with no EMA or momentum teacher** ([JARVIS](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15885)). Its layer sweep is sharp: vision-centric average 50.4 at j=4, **51.7 at j=8**, 50.3 at 16, 50.2 at 24, 49.2 at 31 and 49.4 at 32 against a 50.9 baseline, so only the quarter-depth reading beats the base model. Cosine beat smooth-L1 (51.7 vs 49.1) and a linear projector fell below baseline (49.6). JARVIS also reports that VIRAL-style same-position alignment and its own "w/o masking" variant "do not improve over LLaVA on vision-centric tasks" in its setup, which is a warning that the predictive/masked framing, not just the teacher, carried the gain.

Two further works bracket the JE-xattn loss from either side. **Latent Denoising** corrupts projected visual tokens, reads hidden states at **LLM layer 16 of 32 (layer 16 > 8 > 32 in its ablation)** and regresses them onto the frozen front-end vision encoder's clean patch features with a normalised MSE, adding a relational KL over intra-image cosine rows and an intra-image InfoNCE explicitly "to prevent representation collapse"; it improves 17–18 of 18 benchmarks on LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen2.5-VL-7B but evaluates nothing spatial ([Latent Denoising](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21343v2)). **GeoWorld-VLM** aligns post-projector image features (what a *frozen* LLM consumes) to a frozen video world model with cosine loss at λ_align = 0.10 plus a preservation loss at 0.05 to a frozen copy of the original vision side; swapping the teacher to VGGT scores 63.65 vs 65.45, and plain DINOv3 distillation *lowers* InternVL3.5-2B from 57.06 to 54.81, which the authors read as "static feature distillation may be insufficient, or even harmful" ([GeoWorld-VLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16713v2)). The only precedent for "the target is a stop-gradient copy of something the model itself produces" is **PRe**, which regresses middle-layer visual states onto the detached pre-LLM projected features through a 2-layer MLP at λ = 0.5, finds λ = 2.0 degrades, and argues that external targets such as DINOv2 "may introduce conflicting objectives" ([PRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20808)).

Finally, the "aligned key space" idea has an older cousin. BLIP-2's stage-1 image-text contrastive loss aligns the Q-Former's *query outputs*, which later become the LLM prefix, and removing that stage gives "substantially lower performance on zero-shot VQA" with catastrophic forgetting on OPT ([BLIP-2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.12597)); Grounded 3D-LLM's CLASP matching in a shared text space moves ScanRefer Acc@0.25 from 6.5 to 47.9 ([Grounded 3D-LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.10370)). Both align the tokens that will be *consumed* by the LLM against language, not against the LLM's own query states, and neither touches the cross-attention key projection. A search of 2022–2026 fusion architectures found no paper that trains cross-attention keys with a contrastive or predictive loss against the LLM query space, and none that uses "does visual token i retrieve its own region's key" as a training monitor; the closest diagnostics are Flamingo's per-layer gate-magnitude curves and 3DRS's cross-view voxel-correspondence cosine, which it shows correlates with downstream accuracy ([Flamingo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.14198); [3DRS](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01946v2)).

## Spatial VLMs add geometry early, and gating decides more than the operator

Across roughly 25 spatial VLMs the dominant recipe is a frozen VGGT (or CUT3R / StreamVGGT / AnySplat), a 2×2 merge plus 2-layer MLP, and **element-wise addition to the visual tokens before the LLM**, trained with next-token loss and a fully-tuned LLM (VG-LLM, Spatial-MLLM, LLaVA-3D, Video-3D LLM). The models that inject *inside* the LLM all use addition and all restrict injection to the earliest layers: SpatialStack maps VGGT layers 11/17/23 to LLM layers 0/1/2 on the visual-token slice ([SpatialStack](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27437v3)); GUIDE injects six VGGT layers into the first six LLM layers with a per-token sigmoid gate and a per-layer tanh gate ([GUIDE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05695)); ViPS adds expert proxies through zero-initialised convolutions at five layers ([ViPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15054)). Cross-attention appears only *before* the LLM with 2D visual tokens as queries and geometry tokens as keys/values (VLM-3R, SpaceMind, Stream3D-VLM, Spa3R), never as Flamingo-style blocks inside the decoder.

The operator ablations disagree, and the disagreement tracks the task. On dense grounding and detection, addition wins: VG-LLM's Table 7 gives **add 36.4 / 78.6 / 38.2 (ScanRefer / Scan2Cap / Video-Det) vs 3-layer cross-attention 34.4 / 75.7 / 35.4 vs concat+MLP 27.7**, the last falling below the no-geometry baseline on ScanRefer ([VG-LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24625v3)), and GAP-MLLM finds gated mixing 50.6 > cross-attention 49.1 > add 47.9 on video detection ([GAP-MLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.16461)). On VSI-Bench-style relational QA, cross-attention wins: Stream3D-VLM's Table 6 gives **cross-attention 65.7 / 66.0 vs addition 63.5 / 63.4 vs concat+MLP 60.8 / 62.1** ([Stream3D-VLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06891)); Spa3R gives 58.6 vs 51.1 for appending; SpaceMind climbs from an InternVL3-8B fine-tune at 63.07 to **66.77 with shallow cross-attention alone**, 68.73 with a camera-conditioned key/value bias, and 69.58 with a camera-conditioned SwiGLU output gate ([SpaceMind](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23075v2)). The one controlled study of nine injection schemes on a single backbone (3D-Mix, a VLA on Qwen3-VL-4B) ranks **gated per-token blending 68.23 > concat 60.42 > base 57.81 > Spatial-Forcing-style alignment 58.85 > cross-attention 56.25 > middle-layer injection 51.82**, with an ungated pre-LLM "Visual Fusion" collapsing to 4.69 ([3D-Mix](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24393)). Concatenation loses to both alternatives in every ablation found, and no paper reports a parameter-matched comparison.

What the ablations agree on is that stability, not the attention mechanism, drives most of the gap. GUIDE's controlled numbers are the clearest: **ungated injection degrades monotonically with depth (3 layers 52.4, 6 layers 50.3, 9 layers 48.0, 24 layers 47.9, all below the input-level baseline of 51.7), while token+layer gates lift six-layer injection to 55.6**; injecting into deep LLM layers (1:4:21) gives 51.7, no gain ([GUIDE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05695)). SpatialStack's own Table 1 shows naive multi-layer fusion at one point (65.15) losing to the best single VGGT layer (66.36), which the paper calls "feature interference rather than synergy," and its full stacked design reaches **67.52 against a data-matched no-geometry fine-tune of 64.76**, a geometry increment of +2.76 on top of a +11 data effect ([SpatialStack](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27437v3)). That decomposition recurs: VG-LLM +2.4 (59.8 → 62.2), VLM-3R +3.2 with CUT3R (57.74 → 60.90) ([VG-LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24625v3); [VLM-3R](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20279v5)), and Cambrian-S reaches the same **67.5 VSI-Bench average as SpatialStack with no geometry encoder at all**, from data plus a latent frame-prediction head ([Cambrian-S](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04670)). "Does Your 3D Encoder Really Work?" finds that removing a 3D encoder's pre-trained weights or its output barely moves LL3DA's ScanQA CIDEr (77.44 → 76.96 → 76.64) ([2506.05318](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05318v2)), and GASP shows a text-only average-object-size prior lifts a LLaVA baseline's absolute-distance score from 0.14 to 0.61 ([GASP](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30231)). Any geometry arm therefore has to be read against a data-matched control, and a 2–4 point effect is the realistic size of the prize.

VGGT in particular carries a known per-task signature. VLM-3R's Table D, on one LLaVA-NeXT-Video base, shows VGGT raising relative direction from 68.9 to 80.8 and absolute distance from 43.6 to 49.8 while **dropping room size 63.7 → 54.0, appearance order 38.5 → 34.5, relative distance 64.9 → 61.5 and object count 70.6 → 68.8**; CUT3R, which keeps temporal structure and metric scale, recovers all four (67.1 / 40.1 / 65.4 / 70.2). The authors attribute the VGGT losses to its permutation-equivariant design and relative-scale priors ([VLM-3R](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20279v5)). Geometry injection also costs general ability: SpatialStack's BLINK-Spatial falls 56.10 → 52.12 and BLINK 61.12 → 55.46; VG-LLM's BLINK falls 55.9 → 51.5 and Video-MME 62.9 → 59.3; and the released SpatialStack checkpoint scores 28.0 on MMSI-Bench against 32.2 for plain Qwen3.5-4B in this repository's runs ([SpatialStack](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27437v3); [VG-LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24625v3); [local README](file:///shared/workspace/povejmo/math/projects/mvp/.claude/worktrees/je-xattn-proposal/README.md)). Where geometry helps most consistently is relative direction (VLM-3R +11.9, VG-LLM +4.1, GUIDE +2.0) and absolute distance (VLM-3R +6, VG-LLM +5.8), while object size is flat or slightly negative almost everywhere.

## Hidden-state alignment works mid-stack at λ≈0.5, and VGGT is a fragile target in VLMs

The MLLM alignment literature has converged on a narrow recipe: a frozen teacher, a 2–3 layer MLP projector on the LLM hidden states at visual-token positions at **one** intermediate layer, negative cosine similarity, a constant weight of 0.1–0.5 added to next-token loss, and the head discarded at inference (VIRAL, VaLR, PRe, ANCHOR, Spatial Forcing, ROCKET, GLaD). The layer sweeps are remarkably consistent. VIRAL's best layer is 16 of 32 (CV-Bench2D/MMVP 59.67/33.33 vs 58.55/30.67 at layer 4 and 54.87 at layer 28) ([VIRAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07979v2)); ANCHOR traces an inverted U peaking at layer 10 (xMMMU 32.8 / 34.2 / **35.6** / 34.7 / 33.5 / 32.1 for layers 6–16) ([ANCHOR](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15085)); VaLR finds the middle (12th of 27) beats both the last and the front (4th), the front being worst ([VaLR](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04476v1)); PRe explains the middle-over-last preference with a logit lens showing deep visual states drifting toward high-frequency token space ([PRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20808)); OLA-VLM's probing of a language-only-trained LLaVA finds depth and segmentation most decodable from **layers 12–24**, with probe quality correlating with CV-Bench depth accuracy at r = 0.98 ([VisPer-LM](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09585v3)). With geometry teachers the optimum sits deeper: Spatial Forcing's LIBERO sweep is **layer 1 94.6, 8 95.7, 16 93.8, 24 96.9, 32 94.8 against a 92.7 base** ([Spatial Forcing](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12276v2)); 3DRS finds the last layer best (62.9) and the 10th-last clearly worst (59.1) ([3DRS](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01946v2)); MVRD's sweep on a 28-layer Qwen2 reads L7 53.9, L14 57.3, **L20 59.9**, last 59.8 against 57.7 for SFT, so layer 7 is four points *below* plain fine-tuning ([MVRD](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10864v1)). No paper in either family places an alignment loss at layers 0–2; the earliest beneficial reading is JARVIS's quarter depth, and every sweep that includes a layer below one-sixth depth finds it worse than the middle.

Loss weight is the one hyper-parameter with flat, reproducible sensitivity. Spatial Forcing's α sweep gives 73.2 at 0 (no alignment, different baseline), 92.2 at 0.02, 92.8 at 0.1, **93.6 at 0.5, 86.6 at 2.5 and 81.2 at 12.5**, with the authors noting a large weight "may destabilize the VLA visual modality"; PRe finds λ = 0.2 marginal, 0.5 default and 2.0 degrading; VaLR sweeps λ ∈ [0,1] and picks 0.5; VIRAL uses 0.5 without a sweep ([Spatial Forcing](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12276v2); [PRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20808); [VaLR](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04476v1); [VIRAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07979v2)). Nobody uses a schedule. On loss form, cosine beats plain regression wherever the two are compared: JARVIS cosine 51.7 vs smooth-L1 49.1; ROSS's plain MSE regression is clearly worse than its denoising objective on both KL-16 and DINOv2 targets; and the two MSE-based LLM-state regressions (GLaD at the final layer, ROSS's ablation) are the weaker variants in their own papers ([JARVIS](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15885); [ROSS](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09575v2)). Spatial Forcing adds a positional embedding to its VGGT targets and loses 10 points on LIBERO-Long without it (94.2 → 84.4), and VIRAL's token-shuffle test hurts the aligned model far more than the baseline, both of which argue for exact positional correspondence between student token and teacher region ([Spatial Forcing](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12276v2); [VIRAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07979v2)).

Multi-layer alignment is where these methods break. VIRAL's layers 14–18 variant falls to 49.62/22.55 from 59.67/33.33 at the single layer 16 ([VIRAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07979v2)); ROCKET shows that with **independent per-layer projectors the cross-layer gradients become nearly orthogonal and LIBERO drops from 96.4 to 80.0** (Goal suite 97.6 → 42.2), while a single shared projector restores 98.2 and 8 aligned layer pairs beat 24 (97.4 vs 94.1) ([ROCKET](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17951)); HeRA finds aligning the worst 10 attention heads collapses to 46.0 from a 54.2 baseline while the worst 5 reach 55.7 ([HeRA](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23885)). GASP is the exception that supervises all 28–32 layers, but with an InfoNCE correspondence head on ground-truth point tracks rather than a feature regression ([GASP](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30231)). The published gains are also fragile to the backbone: in HeRA's reproduction on Qwen3-8B, VIRAL scores 54.2 vision-average against 55.9 for plain LLaVA, and a VIRAL-style middle-layer cosine loss regresses to 53.8 vs 54.2 in HeRA's own ablation ([HeRA](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23885)). Timing conflicts too: OLA-VLM finds the embedding loss helps in pre-training and hurts in instruction tuning, HeRA finds both stages necessary, VIRAL applies it only in LoRA SFT ([VisPer-LM](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09585v3)).

VGGT as the *teacher* is strong in VLAs and mixed in VLMs. In manipulation, Spatial Forcing's VGGT+PE target reaches 96.9 against 94.1 for DINOv2 and 94.0 for SigLIP; GLaD's VGGT teacher scores 94.1 against 86.1 for π³ (and its explicit depth-map supervision "caused training divergence"); ROCKET matches 98.5 LIBERO with VGGT at a claimed 1/24 of the tuning cost ([Spatial Forcing](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12276v2); [GLaD](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09619); [ROCKET](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17951)). In VLMs the picture is different. 3DRS's VGGT target is the best of five on ScanRefer (62.9 vs DINOv2 59.8, MAE 59.1, SigLIP2 58.2) but the paper's VSI-Bench average is only 45.9 ([3DRS](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01946v2)); MVRD's faithful reproduction of 3DRS-style per-token distillation on LLaVA-Video-7B scores **57.0 vs 57.7 for SFT alone, with appearance order collapsing 38.5 → 19.6** even as absolute distance rises 43.6 → 51.3, and 55.5 vs 56.1 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, attributed to degraded vision-language alignment; its relational fix (match the N×N patch-similarity matrix to VGGT's, γ = 0.5, at layer 20) recovers 59.9 ([MVRD](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10864v1)). Render to Reason found that **predicting VGGT latents at novel views reduced VSI-Bench by 1.5** relative to its geometry-augmented baseline while predicting 9-class semantics of the same view added +1.6 ([Render to Reason](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05417v1)); VEGA's encoder-level VGGT teacher collapsed on its hard task (0.04 vs 0.34 with no teacher) where a DINOv2-FiT3D teacher did not, attributed to architectural mismatch with ViT patch tokens ([VEGA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10485v1)). The only VLM result in which a 3D encoder target clearly moves VSI-Bench is VaLR, where π³ as one of three targets on 16 latent reasoning tokens lifts Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 33.0 to 52.9 (DINOv3 alone 41.5), and where latent tokens *without* alignment fall below plain SFT ([VaLR](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04476v1)). Three independent groups (Spa3R, VaLR, Render to Reason) thus found that a geometric teacher works better mixed with or replaced by a semantic one, and two (MVRD, VLM-3R) found VGGT specifically damages order-sensitive sub-tasks.

## Frozen teachers replace EMA in every cross-modal JEPA that worked

The JEPA family prevents collapse with a triad: an EMA target encoder, stop-gradient on the target, and a narrow asymmetric predictor. I-JEPA ramps momentum 0.996 → 1.0, calls the EMA "essential for training JEAs with ViTs," and finds a *narrower* predictor better (width 384: 70.7 vs width 1024: 68.4) ([I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08243)); V-JEPA switches to L1 "which we found to be more stable," ramps 0.998 → 1.0, and stops its schedule at 80 % because the last quarter "updated hyperparameters too aggressively" ([V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471)); V-JEPA 2 fixes the momentum at a constant ([V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1)). Several papers then document that EMA alone is insufficient: C-JEPA calls I-JEPA's EMA inefficacious at preventing entire collapse and adds VICReg terms ([C-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19560)); AD-L-JEPA, despite EMA, loses 2.06 KITTI mAP without an explicit variance regulariser and notes that without per-input averaging "the network learns N constant embeddings" ([AD-L-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04969v2)); LeJEPA removes the teacher-student setup entirely in favour of a single SIGReg term ([LeJEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544)); and a text-JEPA study attributes a reproducible collapse sequence (MI saturation, effective-rank degeneration, cosine collapse) to "centroid degeneracy" when targets admit multiple valid completions ([JEPA Paradox](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23531)).

The cross-modal and 3D JEPAs that succeeded took the other route. **CrossJEPA** predicts frozen DINOv2 view embeddings from point clouds with smooth-L1, no masking and no EMA, and its Appendix A is the most direct evidence on the choice: a variant that added an EMA-updated image branch (momentum cosine-scheduled from 1 down to 0.996) "frequently suffered from model collapse due to unstable gradients from the image-learning branch" and scored 91.73 %, while the frozen-teacher design reached 94.2 % ([CrossJEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18424v1)). **SALT** predicts a frozen pixel-MAE teacher's latents with L1 and states the frozen teacher "obviates the need for both the EMA update and the stop-gradient"; its student quality is "remarkably robust to teacher quality" and the student's latent loss predicts downstream accuracy with R² = 0.951 while teacher loss and embedding rank do not ([SALT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24317)). Spa3R (frozen VGGT + DINOv3), TI-JEPA (frozen I-JEPA and text encoders, freezing "mitigates energy collapse"), Latent Denoising and GeoWorld-VLM all follow the same pattern and report no collapse ([Spa3R](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21186v2); [TI-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.06380v1)). Where the target encoder is trainable, authors add InfoNCE or SIGReg instead: VL-JEPA's ablation shows InfoNCE 23.3/30.3/44.3 (classification/retrieval/VQA) against cosine 16.5/20.2/46.6, L1 14.8/15.5/41.9 and L2 13.5/11.7/43.7, with the note that "only InfoNCE provides anti-collapse regularization when the Y-Encoder is unfrozen," though cosine was best on VQA ([VL-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10942v2)); LLM-JEPA, with a tied LLM as both encoder and predictor and no stop-gradient, finds cosine 71.46 vs L2 2.22 vs MSE 70.64 ([LLM-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14252v2)). Across the whole set, **no MLLM paper uses an EMA target on the LLM side**; the closest are PRe's stop-gradient self-target and JARVIS's frozen external target.

V-JEPA features themselves are not a geometry source. In the V-JEPA 2 paper's controlled VLM comparison (frozen encoders, Qwen2-7B, 18M samples) V-JEPA 2 averages 52.3 against DINOv2 45.7, SigLIP2 48.1 and PE 49.1, but the gain is concentrated on temporal benchmarks and it trails on PerceptionTest (72.0 vs 72.4) ([V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1)). On linear depth probes **V-JEPA 2 ViT-g scores NYUv2 RMSE 0.642 against DINOv2's 0.372**, with V-JEPA 2.1's dense loss closing the gap to 0.307–0.350 ([Dense Spatial Perception](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05247)); on multi-view probes the original V-JEPA beats DINOv2 on CO3Dv2 point maps (0.439 vs 0.559) but trails Fast3R (0.262) and video-diffusion features ([How Much 3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19949v1)). No V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2 or VL-JEPA result exists on VSI-Bench or any 3D QA benchmark; VL-JEPA predicts a pooled text embedding of the answer with no patch correspondence and no spatial evaluation, though it is the one demonstration that Llama layers with the causal mask removed can serve as a JEPA predictor over mixed visual and text tokens ([VL-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10942v2)). The only JEPA in which predicted embeddings serve as attention keys is 3D-JEPA's context-aware decoder, where the *context* representation is the K/V for target mask tokens inside the predictor, not in a language model ([3D-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15803)); JEPA-VLA feeds frozen V-JEPA 2 features as K/V to gated cross-attention every 8 layers of OpenVLA-OFT with an action loss only, and lifts LIBERO-Long from 25.8 to 41.0 ([JEPA-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11832v1)).

## Zero-init gates and full-grid keys are the settled design choices

Gating is the one point where the literature is unanimous. Flamingo's tanh(α) gate with α initialised to 0 makes the model equal to the frozen LM at step 0, and the published ablation is the only number for removing it: **overall 70.7 → 66.5 with reported training instability**; vanilla decoder cross-attention without the FFW branch scores 66.9; cross-attention every layer 70.7, every 4th 68.8, a single middle block 59.8 ([Flamingo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.14198)). LLaMA-Adapter's zero-initialised per-head gate on prompt keys scores **83.85 % vs 40.77 % for random init** on ScienceQA ([LLaMA-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.16199v3)); Llama 3.2 Vision's reference implementation zero-initialises both `cross_attn_attn_gate` and `cross_attn_mlp_gate` and applies them as `tanh()` ([modeling_mllama.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/transformers/main/src/transformers/models/mllama/modeling_mllama.py)); OmniSpace zero-initialises its pose-injection convolution and epipolar-attention output "so the model starts out behaving like the base MLLM" ([OmniSpace](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22617v3)); Spa3R's adapter MLP is zero-initialised; GUIDE, GAP-MLLM, ViPS and 3D-Mix all needed gates or zero-init to beat input-level addition. Flamingo's Figure 6 tracks |tanh(α)| per layer over training and finds all 24 layers open quickly, with magnitudes growing with depth; that curve is the established monitor for "how fast the pathway opens," and nothing in the literature quantifies a safe rate ([Flamingo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.14198)). Idefics2 adds the caveat that cross-attention beats fully-autoregressive fusion only when the LM is frozen (66.7 vs 60.3) and loses under LoRA (67.3 vs 69.5), while unfreezing the fully-autoregressive model caused loss divergence ([Idefics2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02246)).

On what the keys should be, the evidence consistently favours a location-preserving grid over a global latent set for spatial tasks. Honeybee's Resampler scores **43.9–45.6 on its spatial subset vs 52.6–56.3 for locality-preserving projectors at the same token count**, attributing the loss to the resampler attending to "a few regions" ([Honeybee](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06742)); Cambrian-1's Resampler drops OCR&Chart to **27.1 vs 50.1 for concatenation and 55.5 for its spatially-structured SVA** ([Cambrian-1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16860)); NVLM removed the Perceiver after a Flamingo-1.3B OCR fine-tune plateaued at loss ≈ 0.4 with it and reached 0 without, hypothesising that latent attention "may shuffle the spatial information among the image patches" ([NVLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11402)). Idefics2's "64 latents suffice" (71.7 vs 71.2 at 128) holds only on coarse averages; its own image-splitting to 320 tokens adds +2.6 TextVQA and +6.7 DocVQA ([Idefics2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02246)). Flamingo found enlarging the resampler "lead to unstable training" and that letting text attend to all previous images' keys instead of only the last costs **7.2 points (70.7 → 63.5)** from a lack of disambiguation between images ([Flamingo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.14198)); NVLM's 1-D tile tags recover DocVQA 75.4 → 80.9 for the same reason ([NVLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11402)). Chat-Scene's separate 2D and 3D tokens per object (50.2) beating early fusion into one token (46.9) adds that modality-specific keys should not be collapsed ([Chat-Scene](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08168v3)). Explicit positional information on geometry tokens is the single largest effect in 3D-LLMs, but only on localisation tasks: Video-3D LLM's sinusoidal 3D PE moves Scan2Cap C@0.5 from 31.03 to 83.76 while ScanQA EM moves 30.03 → 30.09 ([Video-3D LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00493v2)), and LLaVA-3D's 3D patches add +54.4 Scan2Cap but +0.4 ScanQA ([LLaVA-3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18125)). Relative geometry inside attention (GTA's SE(3) transforms applied to Q, K *and* V) exists only in novel-view synthesis and driving MLLMs, where OmniSpace's epipolar masking beats vanilla cross-view attention on collision rate (1.27 vs 1.96 %) ([GTA](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10375v3); [OmniSpace](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22617v3)).

## What the literature implies for the JE-xattn arm

Read against the arm as specified (2×2-window VGGT keys on Qwen's 20×15 token grid, a 2-layer MLP predictor from the LLM state at visual token i to an EMA copy of the trainable key embedding g_i, L1 on LayerNorm'd targets, λ≈0.5, momentum 0.996 → 1.0, three injection points after LLM layers 0/1/2 fed by VGGT layers 11/17/23, zero-initialised output projections), the evidence sorts the design choices as follows.

| Grid-aligned 2×2 window keys, one per Qwen visual token | supported | Honeybee 45.6 vs 56.3, Cambrian-1 27.1 vs 55.5, NVLM OCR loss plateau; Spa3R xattn 58.6 vs append 51.1; GLaD pools VGGT to the token grid; VIRAL/ROSS resize so grids coincide |
| Concatenating the four patches rather than pooling | unaddressed | no paper compares; GLaD adaptive-pools, SpatialStack 2×2-merges with an MLP; VIRAL interpolates SAM features |
| Zero-init gated output, FFW branch, text tokens as queries | supported | Flamingo −4.2 without gate and unstable, −3.8 without FFW; LLaMA-Adapter 83.85 vs 40.77; Llama 3.2 zero-init tanh |

| Alignment loss read at layers 0–2 | contradicted | every sweep: JARVIS j=4 50.4 < j=8 51.7; ANCHOR L6 < L10; VaLR front worst; MVRD L7 53.9 < SFT 57.7; 3DRS 10th-last worst; Spatial Forcing L1 94.6 < L24 96.9; OLA-VLM probes peak at 12–24 |

The two most consequential findings concern the target and the layer. On the target, the proposal's own argument for EMA ("if the context side output a constant, the target would keep moving") is the BYOL mechanism, but in every multimodal setting where a frozen teacher was available, authors dropped EMA and were fine, and the one that kept an EMA branch across modalities collapsed. The JE-xattn target has a further complication none of those papers faced: the key embedding E receives gradient only from the answer loss through the keys, roughly 13 one-token answers per video, so Ē is an EMA of a slowly, sparsely trained map and is nearly a fixed projection of VGGT for most of training. That is close to SALT's frozen-teacher regime, in which the EMA machinery buys nothing, except that the target *can* drift under the attention block, which the proposal itself flags. The literature's answer is to run the frozen-target variant as the primary arm and treat EMA as the ablation: freeze the target at the RMSNorm'd 8192-d window (or at a fixed copy of E after the alignment-only warm-up stage the proposal already plans), keep the keys trainable, and add either AD-L-JEPA's per-dimension variance term or Latent Denoising's relational KL if any gradient is allowed into E from the alignment loss. PRe is the citation for "target = stop-gradient copy of the model's own pre-LLM features at λ = 0.5," which is the closest published form of "keys shared with the alignment target."

On the layer, there is a genuine tension between where injection works (0–2, SpatialStack and GUIDE) and where alignment works (one quarter to three quarters depth, every sweep). The resolution the evidence suggests is to decouple them: inject at 0–2 as planned, but read the alignment loss once, at a single layer between roughly 8 and 14 of Qwen3.5's stack, through a single shared predictor, with the three key sets from VGGT 11/17/23 serving as targets only through one of them (Spa3R's Table IV and SpatialStack's Table 1 both point at the deeper VGGT layer 23 as the most useful alone). At layers 0–2 the visual-token hidden state is barely transformed from the projector output, so the proposed loss is in effect GeoWorld-VLM's post-projector alignment, which worked with λ = 0.10, a preservation loss and a frozen LLM, and which found VGGT 1.8 points worse than a semantic teacher. If the three-point reading is kept, ROCKET's result makes a shared predictor across the three points mandatory rather than optional. Early-layer alignment is not expected to be harmful at the arm's weight (Spatial Forcing's layer-1 reading still beat its base by 1.9), only to leave most of the available gain on the table.

On the target modality, the recurring finding that VGGT feature regression damages appearance order and room size while helping direction and absolute distance means those two sub-tasks, plus MMSI-Bench, should be pre-registered as the regression gate; the proposal already expects appearance order not to improve, but MVRD's 38.5 → 19.6 is a collapse, not a plateau. Spa3R's VGGT+DINOv3 mix (58.6 vs 57.5) and VaLR's π³+DINOv3+SigLIPv2 (52.9 vs 41.5) are the cheapest insurance: a second target head on a semantic encoder costs one projector. A learned frame-index embedding on the keys addresses the VLM-3R and Flamingo multi-view findings directly and is the obvious first variant if appearance order drops.

Finally, the expected effect size bounds what a pilot can conclude. Published geometry increments over a data-matched fine-tune are +2.4 to +3.2 on VSI-Bench and SpatialStack's is +2.76; Spa3R's predictive latents added +3.5 over raw VGGT through the same adapter. A 5.6k-question subset pilot with ±3-point resolution cannot rank arms on that margin, so its value lies in the mechanism signals the proposal lists: whether the dev alignment loss falls and tracks training (SALT's R² = 0.951 result says the student's latent loss is the right proxy), whether per-dimension standard deviation stays open, whether retrieval of key i by query i rises well above 1/2,400, and whether a depth probe on the aligned states exceeds the v3 cross-attention checkpoint (Spatial Forcing's probe, OLA-VLM's r = 0.98 probe-to-accuracy correlation). The closest prior works to cite are Spa3R (JEPA-style VGGT+DINOv3 latent prediction whose latents become cross-attention keys), JARVIS (I-JEPA inside an LLM with the LLM as predictor and a frozen target), 3DRS, Spatial Forcing and MVRD (hidden-state-to-VGGT regression and its layer and sub-task effects), PRe and Latent Denoising (self-targets and anti-collapse terms inside an LLM), SpatialStack and GUIDE (early-layer injection and gating), SpaceMind and Stream3D-VLM (grid-key cross-attention beating addition on VSI-Bench), Flamingo and LLaMA-Adapter (zero-init gating), Honeybee, Cambrian-1 and NVLM (latents vs grid keys), and CrossJEPA, SALT and AD-L-JEPA (frozen teachers vs EMA and variance regularisation).

## Conclusion

The JE-xattn arm occupies real white space: the field has an "inject and hope" camp and an "align and discard" camp, and the one bridge between them (Spa3R) builds the aligned latents outside the LLM. That gap is also a warning, because the papers nearest to each half of the design report that the half-measures fail in specific ways: hidden-state regression to VGGT degrades order-sensitive reasoning unless made relational or mixed with a semantic teacher, and alignment losses read too early or at several layers with separate heads fall below plain fine-tuning. The novelty that is cheapest to keep is the one the evidence most supports, grid-aligned keys that double as a dense training target behind a zero-initialised gate; the novelty that is most expensive to keep is the EMA target, which the cross-modal JEPA record says is unnecessary with a frozen geometry encoder and was the component that collapsed when tried.

The more useful reframing the literature offers is that the shared query/key space does not need JEPA's collapse machinery at all; it needs a stationary target, one reading point deep enough that the LLM state has something geometric to predict, and a monitor that says whether query i actually finds key i. SALT's result that the student's latent loss predicts downstream quality, and 3DRS's that cross-view feature correspondence predicts accuracy, mean the arm can be judged on its alignment curves before any VSI-Bench number arrives. If the frozen-target variant at a mid-stack reading point shows retrieval and probe gains and the λ = 0 ablation does not, the design has done something no published model has; if the EMA variant is needed to get there, that itself would be a new finding worth reporting.
