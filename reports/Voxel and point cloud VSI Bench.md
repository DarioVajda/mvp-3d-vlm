# Predicted point maps, not voxels, lead VSI-Bench

Yes. In 2026 several groups fed explicit 3D into VLMs and evaluated on VSI-Bench, but the representation that wins is almost never a point cloud, voxel grid or splat built from sensors. It is geometry predicted from video by a feed-forward 3D model (VGGT, π3, CUT3R, Point3R), usually as **latent features**, sometimes with **pixel-aligned point maps** pooled to the visual-token grid. The trained leaders at ≤8B sit at **73–75 average**: SSR-3D-7B (73.9, 32 frames), Cambrian-P-7B (73.7, 128 frames), SpaceMind++-8B (73.2, frames not stated) and GeoWeaver on Qwen3.5 (72.8 for the 5B, 75.05 for the 10B, frame count ambiguous). Explicit grid, BEV and voxel methods top out lower, at **63–65** (Cambrian-BEV-0.5B 63.48 at 128 frames with ground-truth depth; Cog3DMap-8B 65.1 at 32 frames). No Gaussian-splat or point-cloud-encoder (PointNet/PTv3/Sonata) VLM reports VSI-Bench at all. Against that field, the MPhil project (Qwen3.5-2B, VGGT point maps only, about 70–71 average and 86–88 relative direction at 32 frames) is the **strongest ≤3B-LLM result found**. It sits level with 7–8B systems such as SpaceMind and VLM², and about 3 points below the 8B leaders. The tie between its additive and 3D-aligned cross-attention arms matches the published fusion ablations, which favour simple additive injection. Two things limit every number in this report, the project's included. First, scores are not comparable across model sizes and frame counts. Second, the dominant training set, VSI-590K, draws on the same ScanNet, ScanNet++ and ARKitScenes scenes as VSI-Bench, and no leading paper reports the bias-pruned VSI-Bench-Debiased.

## Every 2026 VSI-Bench leader gets its geometry from a feed-forward 3D model

The research turned up no VSI-Bench result for a classic point-cloud encoder. PointLLM, SpatialLM (Sonata plus Qwen2.5-0.5B) and Apple's Pts3D-LLM (Sonata-PTv3 features added to SigLIP tokens) are evaluated on 3D-LLM and scene-modelling benchmarks instead ([Pts3D-LLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.05689); [SpatialLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07491v2)). Every VSI-Bench method in the 3D-input family instead runs a feed-forward reconstruction model on the video frames and uses what it produces in one of three ways. **Latent features only** is the most common: GeoAlign, GeoWeaver, SpaceMind, VG-LLM, Spatial-MLLM and VLM-3R. **Latent features plus pixel-aligned point maps** is used by VLM², SpaceMind++, Cog3DMap and VLM-IE3D. The third way uses **point-map coordinates to build a voxel or point memory**, as SpaceMind++ and Cog3DMap do. The project falls into none of these cleanly: it injects only point-head xyz, with no latent VGGT features. That makes it unusual, because VG-LLM's authors explicitly argued that VGGT point maps are "relatively sparse and may contain errors" and chose latent features instead ([VG-LLM](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2505.24625v1), quote seen only in a snippet).

Two patterns stand out. First, **relative direction in the high 80s is now routine** for any model that injects 3D geometry and trains on VLM-3R- or VSI-590K-style data: 85.6–93.4 across Cog3DMap, VLM², SpaceMind, Cambrian-P, SpaceMind++ and SSR-3D. Untuned Qwen3-VL-2B scores 42.0 ([VLM²](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20644v2)). The project's 86–88 is therefore expected, not exceptional. Second, the 2025 generation of VGGT-feature models (VG-LLM, Spatial-MLLM, 46–51) sits about 20 points below the 2026 generation. The gap tracks the training data more than the architecture, which leads straight to the contamination problem below.

## Grids, BEV maps and scene graphs stall in the low-to-mid 60s

The methods that build an explicit scene structure split into learned token interfaces and training-free agents. Neither beats the learned-fusion leaders. **Cambrian-BEV** is the purest grid experiment. It back-projects DINOv3 patch features with **dataset sensor depth and ground-truth poses** onto a 27×27 ground-plane grid (±5 m, 0.37 m cells, 729 tokens) that replaces the frame tokens, with no new parameters and no extra positional encoding. At 0.5B and 128 frames it scores **63.48 against 54.98** for a frame-token control fine-tuned the same way. The gain is large on room size (85.00 vs 60.49) and relative direction (82.67 vs 48.58), but appearance order drops from 76.86 to 62.46 because the time-collapsed grid discards visitation order ([Cambrian-BEV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22700v1)). Its ablations bear directly on the project. A **hybrid of frames plus BEV tokens scores only 56.06**, a 2-layer MLP adapter on the BEV tokens drops it to 51.45, and the authors' own frame baseline sits about 10 points below the official Cambrian-S-0.5B, which they attribute to bf16 autocast. The +8.5 gain is therefore measured against a weakened control ([Cambrian-BEV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22700v1)). **Cog3DMap** keeps one token per 3D location in a recurrent memory driven by a frozen Point3R and reaches **65.1 at 8B and 32 frames**, with relative direction 85.6 and route planning 43.0 ([Cog3DMap](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23023v1)).

The agent pipelines show where explicit metric 3D still helps. **DeSpatial** is a training-free Qwen3-VL-8B agent over a gravity-aligned, metric instance map built with Pi3, Depth Anything 3 and SAM3. It reaches **69.22**, with **route planning 64.43**, far above any trained ≤8B model (Cambrian-P 52.6, SSR 48.5). Its frame count for the main result is not stated; its oracle runs use 32 frames. With ground-truth 3D boxes, its five object-centric tasks reach 98.23, against 50.38 for the raw SAM3+Pi3 pipeline, which shows that perception and instance fusion are the bottleneck, not reasoning ([DeSpatial](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20990)). **GeoLift** lifts frozen Qwen3-VL-4B from 55.4 to **64.1** (frames not stated) using VGGT-Omega point clouds plus an occupancy grid searched with A* for routes (route 30.9 → 53.6) ([GeoLift](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33558)). **GR3D** voxelises a π3 reconstruction into labelled objects and lifts GPT-5 at 16 frames from 50.3 to 59.8 ([GR3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08592v2)). **EagleVision** (Dec 2025) gives a GRPO-trained Qwen3-VL-8B a BEV occupancy image plus a pose-query tool and reaches 63.5 (frames not stated) ([EagleVision](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15160v1)). **RieMind** shows the ceiling for scene graphs. Built from ground-truth annotations and restricted to a 6-task static split, its graph lets GPT-4.1 reach 89.5, but a Qwen2.5-VL-7B agent manages only 64.1 and 34.7 on relative direction ([RieMind](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15386)). So small models cannot exploit explicit graphs without training.

The research found **no Gaussian-splat or NeRF VLM evaluated on VSI-Bench**. 3DGSNav targets object navigation instead ([3DGSNav](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12159)). The closest is VLM-IE3D, which can use AnySplat Gaussians as explicit tokens, scores only 47.6 at about 4B and up to 32 frames, and defaults to depth rather than splats ([VLM-IE3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21595v1)). "GS-Reasoner" (64.7, relative direction 88.9) appears in SSR's table, but its method was not verified ([SSR](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00409)). Voxel-token 3D LLMs such as Qwen-3D (5 cm voxel pooling plus M-RoPE extended to world xyz) and Geo3DPruner (VGGT-assigned 0.1 m voxels) report only ScanRefer-style benchmarks, not VSI-Bench ([Qwen-3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02980); [Geo3DPruner](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18260)).

## Published fusion ablations predict the additive–cross-attention tie

The tie between the project's two arms is what the literature predicts. **GeoAlign** compared five fusion operators at 4B and found shared projection plus addition best: 71.4, against 70.1 for FiLM, 70.7 for 2D gating and 69.4 for split projection ([GeoAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12630)). **GeoWeaver** reaches the highest score found with a purely additive, zero-initialised injection. Its ablation varies *where* the injection happens rather than how: 68.10 at input level, 70.45 decoder-side and 72.80 at its "pre-reasoning" placement for the 5B ([GeoWeaver](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22558v1)). That is the most relevant control for the project's choice of layers 0–2, since GeoWeaver also builds on Qwen3.5. **VLM²** found gated point-map injection (61.6) better than ungated (58.9) and better than VGGT cross-attention fusion (60.2), and concat-plus-MLP far worse (40.4–43.3), against a 55.2 base ([VLM²](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20644v2)). **Cog3DMap** found, on Scan2Cap rather than VSI-Bench, that adding learnable PE, 4D-RoPE or HRoPE on xyz *on top of* additive geometry features slightly hurts (79.4 → 76.6–78.0) ([Cog3DMap](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23023v1)).

The cross-attention arm has partial prior art. **SpaceMind++ applies 3D RoPE on VGGT point-map coordinates to Q and K** in both map self-attention and map→visual cross-attention, but it voxelises first, adds a data change (900K samples) at the same time, and never ablates against additive fusion ([SpaceMind++](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09449v1)). Qwen-3D reports, on ScanRefer at 3B, that naive 3D positional encoding hurts (49.9 vs 53.2 for 2D PE) while an "aligned 3D" variant recovers it (53.5) ([Qwen-3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02980)). The research found **no paper using an ALiBi-style metric-distance bias (−ν·dist) over reconstructed points**, and no VSI-Bench additive-versus-cross-attention comparison at matched model, data and frames. The project's controlled two-arm design, at 32 frames on one backbone, is therefore a genuinely new data point. Its null result ("structure in attention adds nothing over additive coordinates") agrees with GeoAlign, Cog3DMap and GeoWeaver rather than contradicting them.

## A 2B point-map-only model sits level with 7–8B feature models

Measured against the table, the project at about 70–71 (32 frames) clearly beats every ≤4B-LLM result found. It beats Cambrian-S-3B (57.3), Spatial-MLLM-4B (48.4), VG-LLM-4B (47.3) and VLM-IE3D-4B (47.6) ([Cambrian-S](https://cambrian-mllm.github.io/cambrian-s/); [Cog3DMap table](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23023v1)). The one exception is GeoAlign-4B (71.4, frames unstated). GeoAlign's "4B" counts its VGGT encoder on a 3B LLM, and the project also runs VGGT at inference, so the like-for-like comparison is a 3B LLM against a 2B LLM. At 32 frames, the only exact frame-count matches are SpaceMind-8B (69.6 or 70.2), Cog3DMap-8B (65.1) and SSR-3D-7B (73.9). The project beats or equals the first two with a quarter of the LLM and trails SSR by about 3 points. Its relative direction of 86–88 equals SpaceMind's (88.4) and VLM²'s (87.8) but is below SSR-3D's 93.4 and GeoWeaver-10B's 92.0.

The most interesting scientific point is the input. Most evidence says latent 3D features carry more signal than raw coordinates. In VLM-IE3D, implicit tokens beat explicit ones on 3D detection (40.5 vs 34.7 F1, both 42.8) ([VLM-IE3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21595v1)). In Cog3DMap, additive latent geometry beats xyz encodings by 8–15 CIDEr ([Cog3DMap](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23023v1)). GeoWeaver lists its lack of point maps as a limitation, yet it still leads ([GeoWeaver](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22558v1)). Reaching 70–71 with coordinates alone is a data point against the view that latent features are necessary. It is confounded, however, by Qwen3.5's base strength, which has no official VSI-Bench number (the Qwen3.5-2B card reports ERQA and EmbSpatialBench only ([Qwen3.5-2B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B))), and by the training data. The cleanest controls are a no-3D Qwen3.5-2B arm trained identically and a latent-VGGT additive arm. SSR's 2D→3D gap is the published reference for how large the first gap should be: +2.0 average, +5.5 relative direction, +3.7 absolute distance at 7B and 32 frames ([SSR](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00409)). Task-level headroom is predictable: route planning stays at 43–56 for every trained model, and only explicit planners (DeSpatial's 64.43, GeoLift's A* at 53.6) break out of it.

## Shared scenes with VSI-590K undercut every leaderboard comparison

**VSI-590K draws on ScanNet, ScanNet++ V2 and ARKitScenes, the same source datasets as VSI-Bench's videos, and the Cambrian-S release makes no statement about overlap** ([Cambrian-S](https://cambrian-mllm.github.io/cambrian-s/)). That training set, or data built from the same scenes, feeds most of the leaders. SpaceMind++ trains on a 900K mix that includes VSI-590K-Video ([SpaceMind++](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09449v1)). Cambrian-P trains on VSI-590K alone ([Cambrian-P](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22819)). Cambrian-BEV's VSI-288K-Depth covers 4,955 scenes from the same three datasets ([Cambrian-BEV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22700v1)). SSR is the only leader that explicitly says it held VSI-Bench video sequences out of training ([SSR](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00409)). The project's own commit history ("Add warmup-stable-decay training with decay branches and VSI-590K data") indicates that VSI-590K is in its training mix too, so it shares the same exposure. Its ~70–71 should be read as an in-distribution score until shown otherwise.

**VSI-Bench-Debiased** (Brown et al.) addresses part of this. It prunes samples that a blind model can solve and keeps about 70% of them. In summaries of the paper, object size turns out to be dominated by a category-mean-size prior, and a blind LLaVA-Video-7B fine-tuned on in-domain data rises from 25.9 to 44.7 on the original benchmark but reaches only 32.0 on the Debiased set. These figures come from summaries and were not checked against the PDF ([VSI-Bench-Debiased](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04655v1)). **None of Cambrian-P, SpaceMind++, SSR, Cog3DMap or DeSpatial reports Debiased numbers** in the parts read. **ReVSI** re-annotates VSI-Bench with tighter boxes and corrected orientations ([ReVSI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24300)). Cambrian-BEV (44.68 vs 40.17) and GeoWeaver report it, and DeSpatial cites it to caution that VSI-Bench labels are imperfect ([DeSpatial](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20990)). Reporting Debiased and ReVSI scores, plus per-task blind baselines for object size and room size, would put the project ahead of most of the published field on rigour, at little cost.

## Conclusion

The 2026 literature reframes the project's question. "Explicit versus implicit 3D" turns out to matter less than three other things: *where* geometry enters the decoder, *how little* new machinery the interface adds, and *what scenes the model was trained on*. Grids and voxels buy token efficiency (Cambrian-BEV's 12.6× fewer tokens) and explicit planning (route planning) but lose temporal order and have not reached the leaders. Attention-level 3D structure, whether 3D RoPE or a distance bias, has not beaten plain addition in any controlled comparison, the project's included. The project's real contribution is therefore not its average score but its controls. It is the first matched additive-versus-3D-aligned cross-attention comparison on VSI-Bench, and it shows that coordinates alone, without latent VGGT features, put a 2B LLM in the 7–8B band.

To make that claim hold up, the next steps follow from the gaps above. The project should add a no-3D Qwen3.5-2B arm and a latent-VGGT additive arm on the same data. It should report VSI-Bench-Debiased and ReVSI alongside the standard score, and state VSI-590K's scene overlap explicitly. A third experiment the field has not run would be to pool the same VGGT points into a BEV or voxel grid on the same backbone, the matched voxel-versus-point-map comparison that no published paper has made.
